## light | Sunlight, drop, eye
Ever wondered why a rainbow always appears opposite the sun? [sun] It starts with plain white sunlight. [drop] That light enters a raindrop, and here is the trick: slows down, bends, bounces off the back of the drop and bends again on the way out. [eye] A fraction of it ends up in your eye. [spectrum] And because every colour bends by a slightly different angle, white light is spread out into a whole spectrum.

## ex_prism | Everyday analogy
Let's make that stick. [ana] Think of a mixing desk that splits a song into bass, mids and treble. A raindrop does the same with light: it sorts it by wavelength. [b1] Red light bends the least. [b2] Violet bends the most. [b3] And millions of drops, each showing you one slice of the spectrum, add up to the arc you see. [merk] To remember the order of the colours, use the classic mnemonic: Roy G. Biv. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

## recap | Takeaways
Quick recap. [r1] Sunlight is a mix of all colours. [r2] A raindrop bends, reflects and bends again, and each colour takes a different angle. [r3] Millions of drops together paint the arc. [m] Remember: Roy G. Biv. [ende] Now go and look for one after the next shower.
