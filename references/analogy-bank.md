# Analogy bank – for the calibration test and for writing analogy scenes

Use these as *starting points*; always adapt to the learner’s home turf. Offer the same concept through 3 domains and let them pick.
Good analogies map **structure** (what depends on what), not surface; say where the analogy breaks.

| concept | cooking | sports | music | gaming | travel / navigation | building / LEGO |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **modularity** (clear parts, simple interfaces) | a mise-en-place station: every pan has one job | a football team with positions | a band: each instrument its own part | character classes in a party | luggage cubes in a suitcase | LEGO bricks with standard studs |
| **feedback loop** | tasting while you cook and adjusting | a coach’s correction after each rep | tuning by ear against a tone | health bar → you heal | navi recalculating after a wrong turn | spirit level while hanging a shelf |
| **trade-off** (cost vs benefit) | spicier = more flavour, less guests | speed vs endurance | volume vs clarity | damage vs defence | fast vs cheap vs scenic route | strong vs light |
| **hierarchy / zoom** | menu → recipe → knife cut | season → match → play | album → song → bar | world map → level → room | country → city → street | house → room → socket |
| **bottleneck** | one oven for ten dishes | the slowest relay runner | one drummer for the whole band | boss with a single weak spot | border control queue | the narrowest pipe |
| **iteration** | tasting rounds before serving | drills → game → review | rehearsals | try → fail → respawn | route re-planning | prototype brick-model |
| **abstraction** (hide detail) | “make a roux” instead of every step | “run a 4-4-2” | “play in C major” | an API call = “cast spell” | “take the train to Berlin” | “wall” instead of every brick |
| **technical debt / shortcuts** | skipping cleaning while cooking | skipping warm-up | ignoring a flat string | rushing the tutorial | skipping route check | gluing bricks |

## Domains to offer (and ask about)
cooking · sports/fitness · music · video games · travel & navigation · building/LEGO/DIY · nature & animals · health & medicine ·
money & shopping · school & everyday life · cars & machines · film & stories · gardening · parenting · strategy/military · own hobby.

## Writing tips
* Open with the familiar picture, then switch: “…and a raindrop does exactly this with light.”
* One analogy per concept; don’t stack three. Mention the limit: “Unlike a recipe, a system can change while you cook.”
* If the calibration shows a domain *loses* (feels forced), retire it for the whole course.
